How the schedule makes it stick
  1. Spacing. Every skill sits in a box. It only climbs a box on a day it is due, after three clean answers in a row. The gaps grow: 1, 3, 7, 16, 35, 80, 180 days. Cramming a skill that is not due keeps it warm but never moves it up. Remembering across a gap is what makes it permanent.
  2. Speed is the test. Right is not enough. The clock goal starts at the drill's target and tightens 6% per box, down to 60% of it. A right answer over the goal does not count toward the run. Second nature means you stop computing and just know, and the only honest measure of that is the clock.
  3. Misses come back. A wrong answer drops the skill one box, makes it due now, and puts it back in front of you two questions later, while the explanation is still fresh.
  4. Interleaving. Sessions mix skills and never ask the same one twice running when there is a choice. Mixed practice feels harder and sticks far better, and it trains the thing you need at a table: recognising which tool the spot calls for.
  5. Recall. Passing a lesson puts its “Commit this” rule on the same schedule. Say the rule out loud before you reveal it, then grade yourself honestly.
  6. Little and often. Fifteen or twenty minutes a day beats three hours on a Sunday. Clear the due list daily; the hours compound because nothing you have learned is allowed to decay.
